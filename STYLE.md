# Visual style

The target look: a detailed top-down military look with soft light, earthy ground, chunky walls and bold tanks. Everything is drawn with canvas code (no image files).

## Principles
- **Light from the top-left.** Top and left edges are lighter, bottom and right edges darker, and shadows fall to the bottom-right (`VISUALS.shadowOffset` in `config.js`).
- **Readable first.** Tiles are about 30 px on a phone (45-60 px on bigger screens). Tanks, bullets and walls must stand out from the ground, so the ground stays calm: small details with low contrast.
- **Bold simple shapes** with a dark outline. Add a detail only if it still reads at about 30 px.
- **Colors come from the theme** (`themes.js`). Draw code mixes theme colors (`mixColors()`, `withAlpha()` in `materials.js`) instead of adding new map colors. Plain black and white are fine for light and shadow.
- **Stable decoration.** Random details use seeded random (`makeRandom`) or a hash of the tile position, never `Math.random` in paint code, so nothing flickers or moves between frames.
- **Patches join up.** Water, forest, sand and ice look at their four neighbours (`sameSides()` in `materials.js`). Inside a patch the tiles run into each other without seams: shared patterns sit at the same place in every tile, and the outline (`tileOutline()`) stays straight with sharp corners on closed sides. Only open sides get a shore, a soft edge or a rim. The edge of the map counts as the same material, so rivers run off the map.
- **The turned playfield stays upright.** In landscape the map is shown turned a quarter clockwise (21 x 13 tiles, the base on the left), but only positions are turned (`mapToScreen()`, `tileToScreen()` in `layout.js`). Every picture is drawn upright, so the light stays top-left and the brick pattern, cracks, shores, smoke and shadows look exactly as in portrait. The neighbours in `sameSides()` are screen neighbours (`directionOnMap()`), so the shores sit on the right sides, and water ripples are offset by the tile's place on screen (`screenCell()`) so they run on across tiles. Only tanks turn: they use the picture for the direction they face on screen (`directionOnScreen()`). Things laid over the playfield as a whole (border, ground shade, vignette, margins and their shadow, snow, hints, banners, the pause button) use its rectangle on screen (`fieldRect()`), and text is never turned.

## Performance rules
- Anything with many steps, gradients or blur is painted once into a small offscreen canvas with `cachedPicture(key, width, height, paint)` (`render.js`) and copied with `drawImage` every frame. Pictures are painted at the tile size times `devicePixelRatio` and are all thrown away when that changes (resize).
- No per-frame gradients, `shadowBlur` or filters on large areas. Gradients and canvas shadows only run inside `paint` functions.
- Big soft layers (ground shade, vignette) are tiny pictures stretched over the playfield; smoothing makes them soft.
- `field.x` / `field.y` and the tile size are whole device pixels, so copied pictures stay sharp.

## How each part is drawn
| Part | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `drawGround()`, `paintGround()` in `themes.js` | 6 looks per theme (`GROUND_VARIANTS`), picked by a hash of the tile position: base color, soft patches, specks, the theme's `details` (grass tufts, stones, sand ripples, snow drifts) and a faint grid line on the top and left edges |
| Ground shade | `drawGroundShade()` in `themes.js` | Big soft patches across the whole field (8 px per tile, stretched), so the ground doesn't look tiled. `VISUALS.groundShade` |
| Brick | `drawBrick()`, `paintBrick()` in `materials.js` | 4 looks (`BRICK_VARIANTS`): shaded bricks, specks, chipped corners, dark joints. Damage cracks are drawn live on top |
| Wall shadows | `drawWallShadows()` in `render.js` | A soft square shadow under each `castsShadow` tile (brick, concrete, metal). `VISUALS.wallShadowAlpha` |
| Tanks | `drawTank()`, `paintTank()` in `render.js` | One picture per look, colors and direction: dark tracks with tread lines, square hull with an engine deck, short thick cannon with a muzzle, round shaded turret with a hatch. The shapes are turned before painting, so the light stays top-left in all 4 directions |
| Tank shadows | `drawSoftShadow()` in `render.js` | A soft square (`VISUALS.tankShadowSize`) under each tank, faded along with the tank |
| Vignette | `drawVignette()` in `render.js` | Darker corners in the theme's `screen` color, over the map and under the tanks. `VISUALS.vignetteAlpha` |
| Water | `drawWater()` in `materials.js` | Three layers. Still water (darker deep patches, light shallows along shores) is cached. Moving ripples and sparkles are cached strips that repeat every 2 tiles (`WAVE_REPEAT`) and slide with `gameTime`, offset per row. The shore (dark wet edge, a little foam, a row of shaded stones in the theme's `bank` color) is cached and only drawn on sides that touch land |
| Forest | `drawForest()` in `materials.js` | A dark floor with 9 round tree crowns on a jittered 3 x 3 grid, each a ball lit from the top-left covered in leaf clumps (lighter toward the light). Crowns reach a little into neighbouring forest tiles (`FOREST_SPILL`), so a big forest is one canopy; on open sides they stay inside, giving a bumpy edge. Drawn at the theme's forest `opacity` |
| Sand | `drawSand()` in `materials.js` | A wavy soft edge on open sides (the ground shows around it), ripple lines at the same heights in every tile so they continue across a patch, grains, lit top/left and shaded bottom/right edges |
| Ice | `drawIce()` in `materials.js` | A frozen lake, flat and level with the ground, filling the whole tile (square corners, nothing raised). Faint blue clouds, diagonal shine streaks on the same lines in every tile (they run across a patch), a thin crack on about half the tiles, frost specks. On open sides: a thin frost line at the shore, and on the top and left the bank's soft shadow on the ice |
| Concrete | `drawConcrete()` in `materials.js` | 3 looks: four cast blocks (2 x 2) with dark joints, each with light top/left and dark bottom/right edges, a faint stain, small pits and sometimes a chipped corner. Four blocks and no rivets, so it never looks like metal. Damage cracks are drawn live on top |
| Metal | `drawMetal()` in `materials.js` | 3 looks: a steel plate lighter toward the top-left, fine brushed lines, a few scratches, a wide mitred bevel (bright top, light left, dark right, darkest bottom) and a shaded rivet in each corner. Damage cracks are drawn live on top |
| Base | `drawBase()`, `paintBase()` in `materials.js` | A gold star instead of the old eagle, cached: a dark frame with a rivet in each corner, an octagonal steel plate with a lit bevel, a gold ring lit from the top-left, and a faceted star (each arm two facets, shaded by the way their outer edge faces the light) with a dark outline. Colors in `BASE_LOOK` (`config.js`). The destroyed base is the same picture broken (`paintBase(c, s, true)`): everything darker, one rivet gone, cracks across the plate, the ring in four pieces, two star arms snapped off, soot, gold and steel shards and a few embers |
| Tank blasts | `addBlast()`, `drawBlast()`, `drawBlastPieces()` in `explosions.js` | When a tank (or the base, `BLAST_LOOKS.baseSize` bigger) is destroyed, over `BLAST_LOOKS.time` (1.4 s): warm light added to the ground, a white flash, a thin shock ring, sparks as short streaks, a fireball of 7 clouds that cools from white-hot through orange to dark red (a hot one in the middle drawn on top, cooler ones around it), grey smoke drifting to the top-right, and 8 pieces in the tank's own colors thrown up and out from under the fire (bigger and with a longer shadow while high), spinning until they land and fade. Each particle's path is picked once with seeded random (`makeRandom`) and worked out from the blast's age, so there's no update step. Fire stages and smoke are cached cloud pictures (`paintPuff()`), flash and ground light are `drawGlow()` with "lighter"; everything is clipped to the playfield. A wall tile that breaks throws a dust puff and pieces in its own colors (`addRubble()`, `drawRubble()`); bullet hits are small sparks. Muzzle flash, rings, floating numbers ("+8", a kill's score) are in `explosions.js` too; screen shake (playfield only), vibration (VIBRATION setting), recoil and the spawn warning star are in `feedback.js`; bullets are a streak with a faint tail (`drawBullets()`). All numbers are in `FEEDBACK` (`config.js`) |
| Scorch marks | `drawScorchMarks()`, `paintScorch()` in `explosions.js` | Each blast leaves a dark mark (3 cached looks: soot thickest in the middle, blotches, soot rays, specks) on the floor, under walls and tanks. It fades out over `scorchFade` and is gone after `scorchTime` (8 s); at most `maxScorchMarks`, cleared when a level loads |
| Player tank looks | `drawPlayer()`, `playerLook()` in `render.js` | The same cached tank sprites in other color sets (`PLAYER_LOOKS` in `config.js`), plus a cached glow (`drawGlow()`) under the tank. See "Player tank states" below |
| Power-ups on the map | `drawPowerUp()` in `render.js`, `paintBadge()` in `powerups.js` | A cached round badge (metal rim lit from the top-left, dark face, a faint ring and the symbol in the type's color, a soft gloss) floating up and down over a pulsing glow in its color |
| Coins | `drawCoins()` in `render.js`, `paintCoin()` in `powerups.js` | A cached gold coin (dark edge, lit rim, raised star, shine spot) that turns (squeezed sideways) over a pulsing gold glow; each coin has its own timing. Coins on forest tiles are drawn again on top of the forest, so they can be found |
| HUD effect timers | `drawEffectTimers()` in `render.js` | The same cached badges, smaller, with a countdown bar in the type's color (timed power-ups only; the shop armor is an upgrade badge). The HUD coin is the same coin picture |
| Control panel | `drawControlPanel()`, `paintControlPanel()` in `controls.js` | One cached picture per screen size and theme: dark metal (a little of the theme's `screen` color mixed in) with brushed lines, a lit bevel and dark line along the top, engraved seams beside the HUD part, rivets in the top corners of each part, and a soft shadow up onto the playfield. Portrait only: in landscape there is no panel, and the HUD and controls sit on the margin art |
| Joystick (Drag) | `drawJoystick()`, `paintJoystickBase()`, `paintJoystickTop()` | Two cached pictures: a base with a beveled metal rim around a sunken well with four etched arrows, and a metal stick top (dome lit from the top-left, grip ring, highlight, shadow). It's always drawn in the movement part, though a drag can start anywhere: the base sits where the touch started (or as close to it as fits in the part) and the top moves as the finger moves from there. Idle, both show faintly (`idleJoystickAlpha`) in the middle of the control's square (`controlZone.moveBox`) |
| D-pad (Cross) | `drawDpad()`, `paintDpadWell()`, `paintDpadButton()` | A dark cross-shaped well with a dimpled hub, and four metal plates of the same size with a bevel and an arrow. The cross is square, as big as fits in the movement part less the margin. Each plate has a cached normal and pressed picture; pressed = bevel turned around (pushed in), darker face with amber light, thin amber outline, bright arrow, drawn 1-2 px down-right |
| Fire button | `drawFireButton()`, `paintFireButton()` | Two cached pictures: a red cap in a beveled metal ring with a dark groove, a glossy highlight and FIRE embossed (dark edge below-right, light edge above-left). Pressed: darker and smaller cap, sunk into the ring, weaker gloss. As big as fits in its part less the margin |
| HUD display | `drawHud()`, `hudLayout()`, `paintHudDisplay()` | A cached metal frame with corner screws around a dark sunken screen (at most 0.6 times as tall as wide), with the text drawn live on it. Under it is a row kept free for the upgrade badges (`CONTROL_ZONE.badgeRowShare`, `badgeRowMax`), even while it's empty, so the display doesn't move when a badge shows up. (The star emblem plate that used to sit there is gone.) Landscape: the same framed display stands upright at the top of the movement side, as wide as its text needs (at least the control's width, at most `LANDSCAPE.hudMaxWidth`), the text in one column or two at the biggest font that fits (`sideHudSize()`), never stretched; the badge row is under it. Overlay layout: a slim framed strip above the playfield with the text in one row and the badge slots at its right end |
| Upgrade badges | `drawHudUpgradeBadges()`, `drawUpgradeBadge()`, `paintUpgradeBadge()` in `hud.js`, `UPGRADE_BADGES` in `shop.js` | Small square badges like tiny HUD displays: a metal frame (`paintFramedScreen()`) around a dark screen with the shop's icon lit from the top-left. Bullets (brass shell) and tank speed (green chevrons) have 3 tier dots under the icon: amber with a soft glow and a shine when bought, dark sunken sockets with a faint rim when not; they show from tier 1. Armor is a silver shield (held until it breaks), base steel the steel plate with the gold star (held until the next level uses it). One row, centered, in a fixed order; the size fits all four in the HUD part, so a new badge never shrinks the others and nothing reaches the display, joystick or fire button. Nothing shows while none are held. One cached picture per badge, tier and size. `BADGE_ROW` in `config.js` |
| Badge flash | `hudUpgradeBadges()`, `drawUpgradeBadge()` | When a badge changes (bought, tier up, armor broken, base steel used) it flashes brighter for `BADGE_ROW.flashTime` (0.3 s): the same picture is drawn again on top with "lighter" at an alpha that rises and falls (a sine). A lost badge stays for the flash and fades out. Changes are noticed as the HUD is drawn, so a badge bought in the shop flashes when the HUD shows again |

| Margins | `drawMargins()`, `paintMargins()` in `themes.js` | The screen space around the playfield: in portrait above the control zone (sides on tablets and computers, thin strips on phones), in landscape the whole screen, so it also fills the side areas and any extra space in very wide or tall windows. It's dark metal like the control panel (`paintMetalBackground()` in `ui.js` with the theme's `screen` color mixed in by `CONTROL_LOOK.panelTint`: brushed lines, plate seams and rivets). The playfield throws a soft shadow onto it (`VISUALS.marginShadow`, wide on the bottom and right) and gets a thin metal frame. One picture per theme and screen size, at one pixel per CSS pixel; the control panel's top edge and shadow sit right on it |
| Page background | `updatePageBackground()` in `themes.js` | The color behind the canvas (seen while loading or resizing) is the margins' metal color, so it never flashes black. It changes only when the theme does; `index.html` starts with the first theme's color |

Drawing order in a frame: margins, ground, ground shade, floor tiles (water, sand, ice), scorch marks, wall shadows, walls, vignette, coins and power-ups, tank shadows and tanks (not on forest tiles), bullets, ambient snow, forest, then the tanks again faintly on forest tiles (`TERRAIN.forestEnemyAlpha`, `forestPlayerAlpha`), explosions (clipped to the playfield), control panel, HUD, controls (only while playing).

## Tanks
- **Player:** yellow-gold.
- **Basic:** grey.
- **Fast:** red, with a slimmer hull (`look.width` 0.78).
- **Heavy:** dark steel and bulkier. It turns bronze, then rusty red as it gets damaged (`damagedColors`).
- **All tanks:** tracks are always dark, the cannon is the turret color a bit darker, and a tank turns white when hit or while it carries a power-up (`FLASH_COLORS`).
- Colors live in `enemies.js` (`colors`, `damagedColors`) and `PLAYER_COLORS` in `state.js`.
- Sprites never change a tank's size or hitbox: each tank is still one tile.

## Player tank states
All looks are cached pictures. Switching is only a choice of picture, and the glow pulses by changing its alpha. Colors, glow, blink speed and blink window are in `PLAYER_LOOKS` (`config.js`).
- **Normal:** gold.
- **Shield power-up:** blue tank over a soft blue glow that gently pulses. In the shield's last `shieldWarningTime` (2) seconds it switches every `shieldBlinkTime` (0.15) seconds between blue and the look it goes back to.
- **Armor (shop):** bright silver tank (clearly lighter than the grey enemies) with a soft steady glow, and no ring around it. When armor breaks, the old ring-break effect plays with silver sparks and the tank goes back to gold.
- **Both:** blue wins. Near the shield's end it blinks blue and silver, then stays silver.
- **Respawn invincibility:** the whole tank blinks, as before.

## Pickups
- **Symbols:** a wrench in green (repair), a shield in the shield's blue, a metal block in silver-grey (fortify), a clock in orange. The shop's armor card has a silver round badge (a tank in a ring); the armor's upgrade badge is a silver shield. Symbol colors are each type's `color` in `powerups.js`.
- **Badge, coin and motion:** rim, face and coin colors, sizes, glow, bobbing, spin and blink rate are in `PICKUP_LOOKS` (`config.js`).
- **Motion stays cheap:** pickups only move by position (bobbing), width (coin turning) and glow alpha. Nothing is repainted.
- **Before they disappear:** pickups blink in their last seconds as before (`POWERUPS.blinkTime`, `ECONOMY.coinBlinkTime`).

## Ground per theme
- **Standard:** olive earth with dirt and grass patches, grass tufts, small stones.
- **Jungle:** very dark moss and mud, dense grass tufts, mossy stones.
- **Desert:** warm sand with ripples and a few pebbles.
- **Snow:** pale blue-white with snow drifts and a few dark stones.

## Terrain colors per theme
Kept in `themes.js` (`tiles`):
- **Water:** `fill`, `wave` (ripples, foam, shallows) and `bank` (shore stones).
- **Forest:** `dark`, `mid` and `light` for the shaded side, main color and sunlit side of the crowns, plus `opacity`. In the snow theme `light` is white, so the pines get snow on top.
- **Sand:** `fill` and `dots` (ripple lines, grains, shaded edges).
- **Ice:** `fill`, `shine` and `outline` (clouds, cracks, dark rim).

## Screens and menus
Shared art in `ui.js`, in the control panel's style, with every static part cached:
- `paintFramedScreen()` / `drawFramedPanel()`: a beveled metal frame with corner screws around a dark sunken screen. Used by the HUD, the end-screen panels and the shop cards.
- `drawTitlePlate()`: a title on a slanted metal plate (`paintPlate()`).
- `drawUiButton()`: a gold (primary) or steel button in a metal frame with a gloss and an embossed label, plus an amber outline when chosen with the keyboard.
- `drawMetalBackground()`: dark metal plates with seams and rivets, for the shop and the start-up screen.
- `drawStarEmblem()`: the base's star plate, whole or broken.
- `drawMessageStrip()`: a short message on a small framed dark strip sized to the text.

The screens themselves:
- **Level result:** the map is dimmed and a framed panel shows "LEVEL X COMPLETE" on a plate across its top edge. The table follows: earned gold in gold with a small coin, missed bonuses grey with 0, and the totals on a faint gold strip. Then the gold CONTINUE button.
- **Game over:** the broken star plate over a framed panel with "GAME OVER" on a plate, why the game was lost, FINAL SCORE as a big number, the records, and PLAY AGAIN.
- **Campaign complete:** the whole gold star plate over a panel with the title on a plate, the TOTAL SCORE as a big number, and the menu buttons.
- **Shop:** a metal background, a framed header (SHOP and a coin with your gold), and a framed "next level" panel with its enemies. Each item is a framed card with its round badge (`paintRoundBadge()`, like the power-ups: bullet, speed chevrons, heart, silver armor, steel plate with the gold star). Tiers show as pips that turn gold once bought, and the price has a coin; MAX shows in green. The selected card gets an amber outline. START LEVEL is a gold button. In landscape the cards form a grid of 2 or 3 columns, with the header and the "next level" panel side by side above it; a card's name shrinks to leave room for its pips.
- **Pause menu** (`pause.js`): the map is dimmed and a framed panel sits behind the buttons with "PAUSED" or "SETTINGS" on a plate across its top edge. RESUME and BACK are gold, the other buttons steel. The main page shows the records at the bottom of the panel, and under them (below another engraved line) the upgrade badges the player holds, the same pictures as in the HUD, each with its name under it in one shared font size (BULLETS, TANK SPEED, ARMOR, BASE STEEL); the panel grows downward for them, the buttons stay where they were. The settings page shows the controls below the map as before.
- **Pause button:** a small metal button with a dark face and two light bars (`paintPauseButton()`, cached, slightly see-through).
- **Positions:** every button and card sits exactly where the layout functions put it (`layoutScreenButton()`, `campaignButtons()`, `shopLayout()`, `pauseMenuLayout()`, `pauseButtonRect()`), so taps work as before. Screens are laid out around a middle point (`overlayFrame()`): over the playfield in portrait, centered on the screen in landscape, scaled so buttons are at least 48 px tall, and scrolling if they're then taller than the window.
- **Loading screen:** the metal background with the gold star plate and "LOADING..." on a title plate.
- **Start-up screen** (`save.js`): the metal background, the gold star plate, the title in gold, the subtitle on a metal plate, the best score on a small framed strip (`drawMessageStrip()`), and the gold and steel buttons.
- **"LEVEL X" intro and world banner ("World 2: Jungle"):** kept as plain outlined text over the dimmed map on purpose. They flash by between levels, and panels made them feel too heavy.
- **On-map messages:** tutorial hints and "Grab the coins!" sit on a small framed dark strip sized to the text (`drawMessageStrip()`). "ALL ENEMIES DESTROYED" goes on a title plate, kept narrow enough to leave room for the pause button.

## Control panel
- **Fits inside the existing layout:** the panel is drawn within the three parts from `portraitLayout()`. The zone height, the playfield size and the touch handling are unchanged.
- **Landscape:** no panel. The same joystick, cross, fire button and HUD pictures stand on the margin art beside the turned playfield (`sideLayout()`), with `LANDSCAPE.spacing` between them and no extra margin. The playfield gets a little smaller (down to `LANDSCAPE.minFieldShare`) when that makes room for them. When the sides are still too narrow (16:9 phones, 16:10 and squarer screens), the controls are drawn see-through (`LANDSCAPE.overlayAlpha`) over the playfield's bottom corners and the HUD becomes a strip above it (`overlayLayout()`).
- **Margin:** the D-pad, fire button and HUD keep the same margin (`CONTROL_ZONE.margin`, 3% of the zone height) inside their parts, so nothing touches its neighbour, even on narrow phones.
- **D-pad shape:** the cross is square with four equal buttons. A touch anywhere in the movement part picks the nearest arrow by angle. The hit area is the whole part, more than the drawn buttons, so a finger drifting between buttons isn't lost.
- **Drag:** steering can start anywhere on the screen except the fire part and the pause button, but the joystick is only ever drawn in the movement part.
- **Moving parts only:** each frame copies the cached pictures. Only the joystick's position and which D-pad or fire picture is shown change.
- **No debug text in the game:** the orange debug tags over the map (FREEZE, a generated level's seed) and "CLEAR SAVE (DEBUG)" only show with `DEBUG` on. Upgrade tiers are shown by the badges, never as text labels.

## Screen layout
- Chosen only from the game's own window size. `resize()` reads `window.innerWidth` / `innerHeight` (and the safe areas from the helper element) and calls `computeLayout(width, height, safe, ratio, leftHanded)`, which works out every rectangle without reading or changing anything else, then copies them into `field`, `controlZone` and `fireButton`. Narrower than tall is portrait, otherwise landscape. It runs on every `resize` event and from a `ResizeObserver` on the canvas. It doesn't use `screen.*` or orientation events: the window size is enough.
- A size change never resets anything: the level, shop, pause state, save, timers, effects, coins, power-ups and enemies go on as they were. Only the input is let go (`releaseForNewLayout()`: joystick, fire button, shop and menu scrolling, and held arrow keys), since the controls, and in a switch between portrait and landscape the directions, may have changed. A held key counts again once it is pressed anew: its auto-repeat is ignored until then (`cancelledKeys`). The cached pictures are painted again for the new size (`cachedPicture()` starts over when the tile size times the pixel ratio or the screen size changes). Nothing about the layout is worked out per frame.
- The turned playfield: the map stays 13x21 (`GRID_COLS` x `GRID_ROWS`) and the game logic, positions, hitboxes and saves never change. Only drawing and input go through the mapping in `layout.js`:
  - `field.turned` is true in landscape and overlay; `field.cols` / `field.rows` are the tiles across and down on screen (13 x 21, or 21 x 13 when turned). `fieldRect()` is the playfield's rectangle on screen.
  - `mapToScreen(x, y)` turns a map position (in tiles) into screen pixels: in portrait `field.x + x * s, field.y + y * s`; turned a quarter clockwise `field.x + (GRID_ROWS - y) * s, field.y + x * s`, so map up is screen right, map right is screen down, map down is screen left and map left is screen up: the base (row 20) is on the left, the spawns (row 0) on the right. `tileToScreen(col, row)` is the top-left corner of a tile's square on screen, `screenCell(col, row)` its column and row on screen. All drawing of things on the map uses them: tiles, wall shadows, tanks, bullets, coins, power-ups, explosions, blasts and scorch marks.
  - Pictures are never turned: tiles, cracks, brick patterns, smoke and light are drawn upright, so the light stays top-left. Only things with a direction turn with the mapping: tanks are drawn facing `directionOnScreen(tank.facing)` (a picture already painted for that direction). Tile edges that depend on the neighbours look up the screen neighbour (`directionOnMap()` in `sameSides()`).
  - Input: keys, joystick and cross give a direction on screen; `currentDirection()` turns it back with `directionOnMap()` (screen right is map up, screen down is map right, screen left is map down, screen up is map left). This is the only place input is mapped.
  - Things placed on the screen, not the map, use `fieldRect()`: the border, ground shade, vignette, margins and their shadow, the pause button, snow, the tutorial hints, "ALL ENEMIES DESTROYED", the level intro and world banner. Text is never turned.
- `controlZone.mode`:
  - `"portrait"` (`portraitLayout()`): exactly as before, the control zone along the bottom in three parts, the playfield not turned. Checked against the previous version: every position, size and pixel is the same.
  - `"landscape"` (`sideLayout()`): the playfield, turned (21 x 13 tiles on screen), uses the full height less `LANDSCAPE.spacing` (8 px), centered. If the controls and the HUD don't fit beside it, the playfield gets one device pixel per tile smaller at a time (`sideLayoutAt()` with a smaller `maxTile` for `fitField()`), down to `minFieldShare` (85%) of its full-height size, and stays centered with margin art above and below; the first size that fits is used. Most phones held sideways get this: 800x360 at 25 px tiles instead of 26, 844x390 at 27 instead of 28, 852x393 at 27 instead of 29, and 1920x1080 at 78 instead of 81. There is no control zone (`controlZone.zone` is null), and the margin art fills the whole screen. The joystick or cross square (`moveBox`) sits in the bottom corner of the movement side, the fire button's square (`fireBox`) in the same place on the other side (swapped by left-handed), each at most `maxFieldGap` (0.5) control widths from the playfield's frame and `bottomMargin` above the bottom. A control is as big as the side allows, at most `controlShare` of the height and `controlMax`. The HUD is a framed display at the top of the movement side with the badge row under it: `sideHudSize()` picks the biggest font from `hudFontMax` down to `hudFontMin`, in one column or two (`SIDE_HUD`). The touch parts are the side areas from halfway between the HUD and the control down to the bottom.
  - `"overlay"` (`overlayLayout()`): used when the side areas are too narrow even with the smallest allowed playfield, i.e. the drawn joystick would be under `minControl` (96 px) or the HUD font under `hudFontMin`. That is 16:9 and 18.5:9 phones held sideways (667x375, 640x360, 740x360), 16:10 and squarer screens (1440x900, 1280x800, 1024x768, 600x600). Their overlay playfield is about as big as a side layout's would be, since the HUD strip takes some height. A slim HUD strip runs along the top (one row of text, with the badge slots at its right end), the turned playfield sits under it, and the controls are drawn see-through (`overlayAlpha`) over the playfield's bottom corners, `overlayControlShare` of its shorter side.
- Very wide or very tall windows: the playfield stays centered, the rest is margin art, and the controls stay next to the playfield.
- The pause button is always at the playfield's top-right corner, drawn at least `pauseMinSize` in landscape, and its tap area is at least 48 px.
- Overlay screens (level result, game over, campaign complete, the continue screen, the pause menu and settings) are laid out around a middle point in units (`overlayFrame(top, bottom)`, with their extents in tiles):
  - Portrait: over the playfield in tiles, as before.
  - Landscape and overlay: centered on the screen, in a unit (`controlZone.overlay.unit`) the size an upright playfield's tiles would have (`uprightTileSize()`: the height less the spacing, over `GRID_ROWS`; so menus and the shop don't grow with the turned playfield's bigger tiles), but at least `minTouch / SMALLEST_MENU_BUTTON`, so the smallest menu button is 48 px, and no wider than the screen. The shop's unit is worked out the same way. A screen that is then taller than the window scrolls (`overlayScroll`): drag it or use the mouse wheel, and a button chosen with the keyboard is scrolled into view (`scrollOverlayTo()`). A tap that turns into a drag presses nothing. The scroll goes back to the top when the screen or the pause page changes.
- In landscape (and overlay) the pause menu (both pages), the level summary, the game over screen and "CAMPAIGN COMPLETE" are two-column panels instead (`fitTwoColumnPanel()` in `layout.js`), so they fit without scrolling. The panel is `LANDSCAPE.panelWidth` (17) units wide (a screen can pass its own `width` and the left column's share, `split`), centered on the screen, with the title plate on top; title and panel together are at most `panelShare` (92%) of the screen's height. Two columns are used only where that width is at most 92% of the screen's width (`isTwoColumnLayout()`): in nearly square windows (600x600, 520x500) the columns would be too narrow for the text at 14 px, so those keep the one-column screens, which fit their taller window without scrolling. If a panel is taller than 92%, text and spacing get smaller together (`panelSizes(k)`), in 1% steps down to `panelMinScale` (75%), with text never under `minText` (14 px) and buttons never under `minTouch` (48 px). Only if it still doesn't fit the screen does it scroll like the other screens. Each column is centered in the taller one's height. Everything is worked out from the current screen size whenever the screen is drawn or tapped, so a resize or a turn of the phone moves it without closing or resetting anything.
  - Pause menu (`pauseColumns()` / `drawPauseColumns()` in `pause.js`): "PAUSED" on top; left the best score and highest level, then a divider and the held upgrade badges in a list, each with its name; right the buttons stacked: RESUME (gold, 1.8 units tall), RESTART LEVEL, SETTINGS (1.4 units), and with `DEBUG` CLEAR SAVE.
  - Settings page (`settingsColumns()` / `drawSettingsColumns()` in `pause.js`): "SETTINGS" on top; left SOUND, SOUND EFFECTS and ENGINE SOUND, right (55% of the width, its labels are longer) CONTROLS and MOVE CONTROL, and the gold BACK centered under both; Up/Down goes through them in that order. The controls are still drawn behind the panel, so a change shows right away: with the controls beside the playfield the panel stays between them (`settingsPanelWidth()`), but at least `LANDSCAPE.settingsMinWidth` (15.5) units wide so "CONTROLS: RIGHT-HANDED" keeps 14 px; in the overlay layout they're under the panel, as before.
  - Level summary (`summaryColumns()` / `drawSummaryColumns()`; the rows come from `summaryRows()`, shared with portrait): "LEVEL X COMPLETE" on top; left one line each for enemies destroyed, gold from coins and the three bonuses (missed ones greyed out with 0), all in one font size that fits both columns (`panelRowFont()`); right "Gold this level" and "Your gold" on the faint gold strip, and CONTINUE under them (`layoutScreenButton()` places the tap area there).
  - Game over (`gameOverColumns()` / `drawGameOverColumns()`; the buttons' labels and actions come from `gameOverButtonItems()`, shared with portrait): "GAME OVER" on top; left the broken star plate with the final score beside it, "Your base was destroyed" (if it was), a divider and the records; right CONTINUE (if offered) and START OVER, with the status line halfway between them. The right column gets 58% of the width so the buttons' second lines stay readable, and the buttons are at least `minText` * 4 tall (the second line is a quarter of the height).
  - "CAMPAIGN COMPLETE" (`campaignColumns()` / `drawCampaignColumns()`; the buttons come from `campaignButtonItems()`, shared with portrait, and are drawn by `drawCampaignButtons()`): the title on top; left the gold star plate with "TOTAL SCORE" and the score under it; right (55% of the width) "CONTINUE TO ENDLESS" (gold, only with `ENDLESS_ENABLED`) and "PLAY AGAIN", 1.6 units tall.
  - Checked in the browser (in the Playables version, before the SOUND setting added a third row to the settings' left column) with all four upgrade badges, `DEBUG` on, the base destroyed and a 6-digit score: at 640x360, 800x360, 844x390, 915x412 and 1024x768 all five fit without scrolling at full size (pause menu 322 px tall, settings 257, summary 219, game over 233, campaign complete 229; buttons 48-64 px, every text and button label at least 14.4 px with the longest setting labels). The settings panel never covers the side controls (532 px wide at 800x360, 574 at 844x390). At 800x300 the pause menu shrinks to 75% and fits the screen (6 px over the 92% box because of the DEBUG button); at 800x240 it scrolls, while the game over (92%) and campaign complete (96%) shrink and fit. At 600x600 and 520x500 all of them use one column and fit.
- The shop in landscape is a card grid (`shopGridPlace()`): 3 columns if 3 cards of `shopCardWidth` units fit, otherwise 2, each card at most `shopCardMax` units wide. The header and the "next level" panel sit side by side above it and START sits under it. The unit makes START at least 48 px, and the list scrolls as before.
- Sizes in landscape: the drawn joystick and cross are at least 96 px, every touch target is at least 48 px, with 8 px between controls. In portrait too every button is at least 48 px (`buttonHeight()`; at 360 px wide the 1.4-tile menu buttons would be 37.8 px), and the rest keeps its old sizes.
- With `DEBUG` on, `layoutSelfTest()` runs on load. It computes the layout for `LAYOUT_TEST_SIZES` (360x800, 412x915, 800x360, 915x412, 768x1024, 1024x768, 1920x1080, 3440x1440, 360x1280, 1280x360, 600x600, 520x500, and the phones held sideways 844x390, 932x430, 667x375), right- and left-handed, and logs every rectangle (a collapsed `[Layout] 915x412: landscape, OK` group with a table, and a line like "playfield 21x13 tiles (turned), tile 27 px (28 at full height), ..."). ## Levels
- Format (`levels.js`): each level is `{ name, theme, world, map, enemies }`. `map` is `GRID_ROWS` strings of `GRID_COLS` letters (13x21, set in `config.js`), legend `.` empty, `B` brick, `C` concrete, `M` metal, `W` water, `F` forest, `S` sand, `I` ice. `enemies` lists type names from `enemies.js` in spawn order; every 4th spawn flashes and drops a power-up (`POWERUPS.flashingEnemyEvery`).
- The game places these itself, and the map must keep them `.`: the base (`BASE_TILE`, column 6, row 20) and its brick wall (row 19 columns 5-7, row 20 columns 5 and 7), the player start (`PLAYER_START`, column 4, row 20) and the spawn points (row 0, columns 0, 6, 12). `loadLevel` clears those tiles anyway.
- `checkLevels()` in `map.js` runs on load, only when `DEBUG` is true, and warns (`console.warn`) for each level about: wrong map size, unknown letter, theme or enemy type, a blocked fixed tile, a spawn point that can't reach the base (flood fill with `floodFill` from `levelgen.js`; brick open, concrete/metal/water blocked), and more than `LEVEL_MAX_FILLED` (35%, `config.js`) of the tiles brick/concrete/metal/water. All 20 levels pass.
- The level name shows under "LEVEL X" in the intro.
- Map design rules: symmetric left/right (small asymmetric touches are fine); the base must be reachable from every spawn point (brick counts as passable, water/concrete/metal do not); water as rivers or ponds (never single tiles) with at least 2 crossings; at most about 35% of the tiles filled (forest, sand, ice don't count); keep the area around the base and the player start open. Enemy types are spread through the list; difficulty rises from level to level.
- Level plan (all done). After level 20 the "CAMPAIGN COMPLETE" screen shows (see "Campaign complete"), then endless mode continues with generated levels (see "Endless mode").
  - World 1, standard: 1 First Steps (10), 2 Crossfire (12), 3 The River (13), 4 Steel Wall (14), 5 Fortress (16)
  - World 2, jungle: 6 Deep Green (14, big forest patches, 3 metal blocks in front of the base), 7 Riverbanks (15, two rivers with forest banks, 3 crossings, 3 metal blocks in front of the base), 8 Hidden Giants (16, 3 heavy, concrete hidden in forest), 9 The Maze (17, brick/forest maze, a pond), 10 Jungle Heart (18, 6 heavy, river, forest center, metal on the flanks)
  - World 3, desert (more fast tanks): 11 Dunes (16, big sand patches, few walls), 12 Ruins (17, concrete/brick ruins, open lines), 13 The Oasis (17, center pond split by a sand path, sand around), 14 Sandstorm (18, 10 fast, vertical sand lanes), 15 Desert Fortress (19, 7 heavy, concrete/metal fortress)
  - World 4, snow: 16 First Frost (17, big ice patches), 17 Thin Ice (18, ponds ringed by ice, brick bumpers), 18 Frozen Maze (18, ice corridors, metal/brick walls), 19 Pine Forest (19, 7 heavy, ice and forest, small pond), 20 Final Winter (20, 8 heavy, river, fortress, ice, forest)
  - (numbers in brackets: enemy count)

## Level generator
- `levelgen.js`: `generateLevel(seed, themeId, difficulty, world, name)` returns a level in the `levels.js` format (plus `seed` and `difficulty`), so the game loads it like a hand-made one. Difficulty is capped to 1-15. It is deterministic: all randomness comes from a seeded mulberry32 (`makeRandom`), never `Math.random`. All numbers are in `GENERATOR` in `config.js`.
- Theme extras are data in `themes.js` (`generatorTiles`): `patches` (how many), `weights` (river, pond, forest, sand, ice), `patchSize` and `riverWidth`. Standard: small rivers, few ponds, forest; jungle: large forest patches, rivers (1-2 rows); desert: sand patches, a pond sometimes; snow: ice patches, ponds, some forest.
- Steps, all on the left half (columns 0-6), then mirrored: at most one river first (rows `GENERATOR.riverRows`, one gap of `crossingWidth` tiles away from the middle column, so 2 crossings after mirroring; the gap and the tiles above/below it are kept open), then ponds of 3-6 tiles and forest/sand/ice patches, then wall segments and small blocks with the difficulty's brick/concrete/metal mix and cluster count (`GENERATOR.walls`). Walls and water keep one tile of space from each other. A solid cluster that would push brick/concrete/metal/water over `maxSolidShare` (35%, the base wall included) is not placed. Clear zones around the base, the player start and the spawn points stay empty (the base gets its brick wall).
- Reachability: a flood fill checks that each spawn point can reach the base (water, concrete, metal block; brick counts as open). On failure it tries seed+1, up to `GENERATOR.maxAttempts` (20), then uses an open layout with a few brick pillars.
- Enemies: 16 to 24 by difficulty; basic and fast from the start (`fastShare`), heavy share rising from `heavyShareMin` to `heavyShareMax`. Each type is spread evenly through the list (seeded wobble). Power-up carriers are still every 4th spawn (game.js).
- `levelAt(index)` in `map.js` returns the level (hand-made or generated, cached); use it instead of `LEVELS[index]`.
- `GENERATED_MODE` in `config.js` (false by default, for testing the generator from level 1): level N uses seed `GENERATOR.baseSeed + N`, difficulty N (max 15), and the theme changes every `levelsPerTheme` levels (`themeOrder`, world number counts up). The run never ends.

## Endless mode
- `ENDLESS_ENABLED` in `config.js` (true by default). After level 20 the "CAMPAIGN COMPLETE" screen offers "Continue to Endless": the shop, then generated levels (`isLastLevel()` is false). With false, that screen has only "Play again".
- Level N (21, 22, ...) is `endlessLevel()` in `map.js`: seed `GENERATOR.baseSeed + N`, theme rotating through `GENERATOR.themeOrder` (standard, jungle, desert, snow) every `levelsPerTheme` (5) levels starting with standard at 21, difficulty `min(15, ENDLESS.startDifficulty + floor((N - 20) / ENDLESS.levelsPerDifficulty))` (5 + (N - 20) / 2), name "Endless N", world number continuing from 5. The usual world banner shows on each theme change.
- Enemy speed/fire scaling is the same capped `ENEMY_SCALING` as for hand-made levels.

## Continue on game over
- The game over screen (`GAME_STATE.LOST`, `drawEndScreen()` / `drawGameOverButtons()` in `gameover.js`) has two buttons of the same size (9 x 1.6 tiles), stacked under the panel (tap, or Up/Down + Enter/Space, after `BUTTON_DELAY`), each with a smaller second line (`drawUiButtonTwoLines()` in `ui.js`): "WATCH AD TO CONTINUE" (with the ad badge) and "Retry level X, keep your upgrades" under it, and "START OVER" with "Back to Level 1" (it clears the saved run and calls `newGame()`). The first button that can be used is the gold one. Between the buttons a small status line: "2 CONTINUES LEFT" (only with a limit and more than one left), "LOADING AD..." while waiting, or the failure message. In landscape the screen is two columns, the buttons in the right one.
- The only way to continue is the rewarded ad. While no ad is ready (`isRewardedReady()` in `ads.js`; always in the browser, where there is no AdMob) the continue button is switched off: dimmed, "AD NOT AVAILABLE" / "No continue right now", no tap, skipped by the keyboard; "Start over" is then the gold one.
- All these texts are in `UI_TEXT.gameOver` (`config.js`); `{level}` and `{left}` are filled in.
- The continue button shows only when `canOfferContinue()`: a checkpoint exists, the lost level is level 2 or later (level 1's checkpoint is the same as "Start over") and the run has continues left (`continuesLeft()`).
- Limit: `REWARDED_CONTINUES_PER_RUN` is 1 by default: one continue per run; `null` means no limit. `continuesUsed` is counted and saved either way.
- Checkpoint: `takeCheckpoint()` (`save.js`) runs in `startLevel()` right after the level index is set, before the base steel is used up: `progress.checkpoint = { level, gold, score, upgrades, seed }`. Every level start replaces it.
- Pressing the button calls `requestContinueAd()`, which calls `showRewardedAd()` in `ads.js` (a Promise of true or false; for now always false, AdMob goes there later). Until the answer `gameOverScreen.waitingForAd` is set: `isPaused()` is true, both buttons are dimmed and off, and `Sound.setAdPlaying(true)` mutes all sound; the answer turns it back on.
- The answer (`finishContinueAd()`): exactly `true` -> `continueFromCheckpoint()`: `continuesUsed` + 1, the run saved again (`checkpointRun()`), lives `START_LIVES_AFTER_CONTINUE` (3), gold, score and upgrades exactly as in the checkpoint, then `startLevel()` with the same level (same map and enemy list, repaired base, no shop). Anything else -> "NO REWARD THIS TIME" (a rejected or throwing `showRewardedAd()` -> "AD NOT AVAILABLE") for `AD_MESSAGE_TIME` (2 s); both buttons stay, no continue is used. No answer within `AD_WAIT_LIMIT` (90 s, counted in `loop()` by `updateAdWait()`, hidden time doesn't count) is "AD NOT AVAILABLE" too; a later answer is ignored (`adRequest`).
- `loseGame()` drops the saved run (`progress.run = null`), so closing the app on the game over screen and opening it again does not give a continue. After a rewarded continue the run is saved again from the checkpoint, with the continue counted.

## Economy and shop
- All numbers are in `config.js` (`ECONOMY`, `SHOP`). Coins: basic 8, fast 12, heavy 25 gold.
- What the player holds shows as upgrade badges under the HUD display (bullets and tank speed with amber tier dots, armor until it breaks, base steel until the next level uses it), and with their names in the pause menu. The armor is only there now, not among the HUD's power-up timers.
- Level bonuses: level complete +40, base walls undamaged +25 (no base wall tile lost health to an enemy bullet: `levelStats.baseWallDamaged`, set in `hitTile`), no lives lost +25. The summary shows earned bonuses in gold and missed ones greyed out with 0.
- Shop prices: bullet upgrade 120 / 280 / 500, tank speed 100 / 250 / 450, extra life 200 / 400 / 700 (max 3), armor 100 (hold 1), base steel 60 (hold 1, used up when the next level starts).
- With `DEBUG` on, the `[Economy]` console line is logged after each level; it ends with `[DEBUG-TAINTED]` if P, O, I or L was used during that level.

## Saving
- `save.js`: `Save.save(data)` / `Save.load()` store one small JSON string in `localStorage` under `SAVE.key` (`tankrush_save_v1`), all in try/catch, at most `SAVE.maxBytes`. Nothing saved, storage that fails or broken JSON gives no save and the game starts fresh. Every loaded value is checked (`applySaveData`, `readSavedRun`, `readSavedCheckpoint`, `readSavedUpgrades`); a bad run or checkpoint is dropped.
- Format (`SAVE_VERSION` 1): `{ version, settings: { sound, soundEffects, engineSound, leftHanded, controlType }, bestScore, highestLevel, tutorialDone, campaignComplete, checkpoint, run }`. `run` is null or `{ level, gold, lives, score, upgrades, continuesUsed }`, where `level` is the level the shop leads to (1-based) and `upgrades` is the shop's `upgrades` object. `checkpoint` is null or `{ level, gold, score, upgrades, seed }`; on load it is only taken over together with a saved run of the same level. Change `SAVE_VERSION` (and keep reading the older ones) when the format changes.
- `writeSave()` saves everything; in the shop it takes the run again first (`rememberRun()`), so purchases are kept. Saves happen only on events, never per frame: shop opened, level completed (`updateRecords()`), game over, "CAMPAIGN COMPLETE" shown, a setting changed, and the app hidden (`visibilitychange` in `game.js`).
- Start-up: `init()` sets up level 1, shows the loading screen and calls `loadSaveAtStartup()` (reads the save at once). With a saved run, `GAME_STATE.CONTINUE_PROMPT` shows "Continue from level X" / "New game"; otherwise the "LEVEL 1" intro starts. Continue (`continueSavedRun()` in `state.js`) restores gold, lives, score, upgrades and `continuesUsed` and opens the shop before the saved level; no mid-level state is saved.
- Best score and highest level show on the game over screen and in the pause menu. With `DEBUG` on, the pause menu has "CLEAR SAVE (DEBUG)" and the loaded save is logged.

## Pause and app lifecycle
- `visibilitychange` (`game.js`): when the page is hidden (app in the background, screen off) the pause menu opens if a level is running (`openPauseMenu(true)`, silent), then `setSystemPause(true)` (`pause.js`) stops the loop, lets go of all input, suspends the AudioContext and stops sound-file loading, and the game is saved. When it shows again the loop restarts with the pause menu still open: the player presses RESUME. There is no auto-resume.
- The pause button and Escape open the pause menu. `isPaused()` is also true while `ads.js` hasn't answered.
- A resize while hidden waits until the app shows again (`resizeWaiting`).

## Audio
- The AudioContext must be unlocked by a user gesture. `audio.js` creates/resumes it on pointerdown, pointerup, touchstart, touchend, click and keydown (phones only count the end of a touch as a gesture, so touchstart alone left the first tap silent), plus a silent buffer for older iPhones; it also resumes from the "interrupted" state, but never while the app is hidden.
- Every sound is built in code from oscillators and filtered noise with a short attack and an exponential decay (no clicks), in `SOUNDS` in `audio.js`; a few are played from sound files instead once those have loaded (see the next points). Sound names: playerShot, enemyShot, brickHit, brickDestroy (the hit that breaks a brick; made in code it's the brick hit), concreteHit, metalHit (metal walls and bullets blocked by the player's shield or respawn blink), heavyHit (a heavy tank hit but not destroyed), explosion, baseDestroyed, coin, powerUp (last note depends on the power-up type, passed as the third argument), armorBreak, purchase, cantAfford, levelStart (a ~1.3 s heroic tune: warm square/triangle brass layers in D major, a D bass, snare, a boom and a crash on the held top note; notes from `melody()` / `heldNote()`, which hold instead of fading; played by `setState()` whenever the "LEVEL X" intro starts: after the world banner, on a restart, not on the very first intro before any tap since audio isn't unlocked yet), levelComplete, gameOver, uiTap. A wall's hit sound is its material's `hitSound` (`materials.js`), and the hit that destroys it plays its `destroySound` if it has one (brick: brickDestroy).
- Sound files: `sounds/` has WAV files (credits in `CREDITS.md`) for playerShot, enemyShot, brickHit, brickDestroy and explosion, listed per effect in `SOUND_FILES.files` (`config.js`), and `engine_loop.wav` for the engine (`SOUND_FILES.engineLoop`). `loadFiles()` fetches and decodes (`fetch` + `decodeAudioData`) all of them at once in the background when the AudioContext is created on the first input. Nothing waits for them. An effect plays one of its files once one has loaded, and its sound made in code until then, or for good if the files fail to load (a failed effect file warns, a missing engine loop doesn't: the engine made in code plays).
- Variations: several files in an effect's list; a random one plays each time, never the same one twice in a row (`pickFile()`). Each file plays at a playback rate of 1 +/- `SOUND_FILES.pitchVariation` (4%); the varied sounds also get the usual volume variation.
- File volumes: one per effect in `SOUND_FILES.volumes` (1 = the file as it is), used instead of `AUDIO.volumes` while the file plays; everything after that (effects volume, softener, mute, limiter, sound limit, repeat gap) is the same as for the sounds made in code. Starting balance, measured through the same phone-speaker filter: at 1 the files were 3-9 dB louder than the sounds made in code they replace, so the two loudest were lowered (enemyShot 0.7, brickDestroy 0.6); the engine file was about 12 dB quieter than the engine made in code, so it's at 4.
- Size: the 6 files are 696 KB (the engine loop is half of it), all bundled in the app, so they load from the device in a moment. Until they are decoded the sounds made in code play.
- Chain: each sound -> its volume (`AUDIO.volumes`, or `SOUND_FILES.volumes` for a file) -> `AUDIO.effectsVolume` -> a gentle low-pass (`softenAbove`) -> mute -> limiter (`AUDIO.limiter`, a DynamicsCompressor; it adds about +4 dB make-up gain) -> speakers. The engine goes through `ENGINE_VOLUME` into the same mute and limiter. The volumes were balanced by measuring each sound through a phone-speaker filter (high-pass at 400 Hz): taps and hits quietest, shots, coins and pickups in the middle, explosions and jingles louder, base destroyed loudest, engine clearly below everything.
- All sound is off while the SOUND setting is off (`settings.sound`, the overall switch; the pause menu calls `Sound.refreshMute()`) or `ads.js` hasn't answered (`Sound.setAdPlaying()`, game over screen): `isEnabled()` is false, the mute fades to 0 and the engine stops. The SOUND EFFECTS setting (`settings.soundEffects`) only stops new effects from playing (`effectsSettingOn()` in `play()`), and ENGINE SOUND only the engine. While the app is hidden the AudioContext is suspended (`Sound.setSystemPaused()`).
- At most `AUDIO.maxSounds` play at once, the same sound doesn't start twice within `AUDIO.repeatGap` (40 ms), and shots, hits, explosions and coins vary a little in pitch and volume each time (`pitchVariation`, `volumeVariation`).
- Engine: one set of nodes (sawtooth + square a few cents apart + quiet noise, low-pass, a volume LFO for chugging) made the first time the player drives and reused; only its fade, pitch and LFO rate change. `updateEngineSound()` in `tanks.js` runs every frame from `loop()` (also while paused) and calls `Sound.setEngine(driving, pitch)`: driving = playing, not paused, player alive and steering. It fades in over `fadeIn` and out over `fadeOut` (150 ms). `enginePitch()`: higher per tank speed tier, lower on sand (`speedFactor` below 1), higher on ice (`slippery`). Off on pause, menus, end screens, a destroyed tank, the SOUND setting off, a rewarded ad and the ENGINE SOUND setting off; a system pause silences it at once and it only comes back once the player drives again.
- Engine from the file: once `engine_loop.wav` has loaded, the engine is that file looping seamlessly (`buildFileEngine()`: an AudioBufferSourceNode with `loop = true` -> `SOUND_FILES.volumes.engine` -> the same fade), with the same fades, pause and mute rules; its pitch changes go to the playback rate. If it loads after the engine made in code was built, that one fades out and stops (`retireEngine()`) and the file takes over.

## Debug mode
Set `DEBUG = true` at the top of `www/config.js` and reload (keep it `false` for release). Only then:
- keys during play: `P` bullet tier +1, `O` tank speed tier +1, `I` freeze enemies, `L` finish the level, `N` next level, `B` previous level, `H` generated level (random seed), `J` next theme, `T` test beep
- orange debug tags on the map ("FREEZE", a generated level's seed)
- console logs: gold breakdown per level, shop purchases, audio state, sound files loaded, generated seeds, the loaded save, and every warning (failed save, failed sound file, failed ad)
- "CLEAR SAVE (DEBUG)" in the pause menu
- the level check (`checkLevels()`) and the layout self-test (`layoutSelfTest()`) on load
Any new debug key or log must check `DEBUG`.
